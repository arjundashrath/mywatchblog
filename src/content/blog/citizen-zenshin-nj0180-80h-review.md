There are some watches that you fall in love with the moment you see them, and then there are watches that convince you to buy them after you spend a little too much time reading their specifications. The Citizen Zenshin Super Titanium Automatic falls somewhere in between. A beautifully textured dial, an integrated titanium bracelet, sapphire crystal, 100 metres of water resistance and a Japanese automatic movement. It sounds like Citizen decided to take everything watch enthusiasts complain about and fix it in one go.

The problem is that its Indian MRP is ₹64,900, which is a fair bit of money for a Citizen automatic. However, during Flipkart's Big Billion Days sale, I managed to pick up the grey NJ0180-80H for approximately ₹35,000. That is nearly half its MRP, and at that price, the watch becomes a considerably more interesting proposition.

Having spent time with it, I have slightly mixed feelings. There is plenty to love about the Zenshin, particularly the dial and just how ridiculously light it feels. But for all its impressive specifications, I cannot say it completely blew me away in person. It is a very good watch, just not quite the extraordinary one that the spec sheet had me expecting.

## That Dial Is Something Else in Sunlight

The dial is undoubtedly the biggest visual attraction of the Zenshin, and under the right lighting, it is absolutely beautiful. Citizen has given it a wonderfully detailed texture that catches sunlight in a way that photographs struggle to reproduce. As you move your wrist, the light plays across the surface, revealing different shades of grey and making the pattern look almost three-dimensional. It is the kind of dial that makes you spend a few extra seconds staring at your wrist when you really should be doing something more productive.

Unfortunately, the magic depends quite heavily on the lighting. Indoors, particularly under dim or flat lighting, the grey dial loses much of its character and starts looking like a fairly ordinary dark grey watch face. The texture is still there, of course, but it is far less noticeable. I found this a little disappointing because the spectacular dial you see in promotional photographs is not necessarily the dial you will be looking at throughout the day.

I also noticed that the darker dial and black outer minute ring do not always provide the visual contrast I would have liked, particularly when seeing the watch on some darker skin tones. The lighter dial variants might be worth considering if you prefer a watch that stands out a little more against your wrist. Of course, this comes down to personal taste, but it is one of those things that is difficult to judge from product photographs alone.

One small detail I particularly like is the off-centre seconds subdial. It gives the watch a lovely bit of character without making the face unnecessarily busy. The main dial is already doing quite a lot visually, so having that little seconds hand sitting towards the lower portion of the watch adds something interesting without overwhelming the design. I would genuinely miss it if Citizen had gone with a conventional central seconds hand.

There is one finishing detail worth mentioning, though. The text on the dial appears to be printed over the textured surface, and it does not look quite as sharp or clean as I would have liked when examining it closely. It is not particularly noticeable during everyday wear, but on a watch with a ₹65,000 MRP, I think it is fair to expect a little more precision here. The dial itself is excellent, which makes these smaller imperfections easier to notice.

## Titanium Is Both the Best and Strangest Thing About This Watch

The first thing you notice when picking up the Zenshin is its weight. Or rather, the surprising lack of it. The watch weighs approximately 99 grams despite having a 40.5mm case and an integrated metal bracelet. Citizen's Super Titanium is claimed to be around 40% lighter than stainless steel, and you really can feel the difference.

It is almost confusing at first. We have become so accustomed to associating heavy metal watches with quality that picking up something this light can make it feel a little less substantial than you expect. Your brain sees a proper metal sports watch, expects a certain amount of weight, and then gets something that feels almost suspiciously light.

But that is precisely the appeal of titanium. Once you get used to it, the comfort is fantastic. The watch does not disappear completely on your wrist, but it comes surprisingly close for something with these dimensions. If you regularly wear heavier stainless steel watches, the difference is immediately noticeable, and I can see this being a major selling point for someone looking for an everyday automatic.

The case and bracelet have a predominantly brushed finish with polished accents, giving the watch a fairly understated appearance. I like the overall design, particularly how the bracelet flows into the case rather than looking like a separate component attached to it. The finishing is good, and the watch certainly looks well put together, although I would not describe it as exceptionally luxurious.

Citizen also uses its Duratect surface-hardening technology on the Super Titanium construction, making it more resistant to scratches than conventional untreated titanium. That is reassuring, but I would still be realistic about wear. Brushed surfaces can show marks over time, and no watch is completely scratchproof. I would not buy this expecting the finish to remain absolutely pristine forever.

## The Movement Is Good, but Nothing Extraordinary

Inside the Zenshin is Citizen's Calibre 8213 automatic movement, which gives you a small seconds display, date complication and approximately 42 hours of power reserve. It is a fairly straightforward Japanese mechanical movement, and there is nothing particularly unusual about it beyond the small seconds arrangement.

The 42-hour reserve is adequate for everyday use, especially if you wear the watch regularly. However, it is not especially impressive when longer power reserves have become increasingly common in modern automatics. Leave the Zenshin unworn for an entire weekend and there is a good chance you will be setting the time again on Monday.

I do not consider this a major problem, but it does reinforce my feeling that the movement is not the reason to buy this watch. At its full retail price, I would have liked something a little more interesting mechanically. Longer-reserve movements exist, although they can come at a higher price, and the Zenshin's main selling points have clearly been its materials, dial and overall construction rather than its movement technology.

You also get a sapphire crystal, which is exactly what I would expect at this price, and 100 metres of water resistance. The latter makes it a genuinely practical everyday watch rather than something you need to worry about every time you encounter a little water. Combined with the lightweight titanium construction, these are sensible features that make the Zenshin easier to live with.

Yet after wearing it, I cannot help feeling that the whole experience is a little less exciting than the individual components would suggest.

Personally, I think the lighter blue and green variants are particularly interesting because they give the textured dial a more distinctive appearance. The white version looks like a sensible choice if you want something understated, while the salmon/orange version has a little more personality. I would strongly recommend seeing these watches in person before deciding, because my experience with the grey dial has shown just how dramatically the lighting can change their appearance.

The core specifications are largely the same across these variants, so your decision really comes down to which colour appeals to you. I would not necessarily recommend choosing the most popular colour or the one that looks best in a carefully lit product photograph. Try it on, look at it under normal indoor lighting, and then see what happens when you take it outside. You might end up preferring a completely different version.

## Would I Buy It at ₹65,000? What About ₹35,000?

This is where my opinion on the Zenshin changes quite dramatically depending on the price. At its MRP of ₹64,900, I think it is a good watch, but not one I would rush to recommend. The titanium construction is fantastic, the dial is lovely in the right light, and the overall specifications are excellent. However, the relatively ordinary movement, the less impressive appearance under indoor lighting, and the small finishing imperfections make that price a little difficult for me to justify.

At ₹35,000, though, it is a completely different story.

For that money, you are getting a full Super Titanium automatic with a sapphire crystal, 100 metres of water resistance and a genuinely interesting dial. That is a lot of watch for the money, even if the movement itself is nothing special. I would happily choose this over most Seiko 5 models around the same price, particularly if you value the lighter construction and better crystal.

I recently [reviewed the Seiko SRPL57K1](/blog/seiko-srpl57k1-review/), and while I genuinely love that watch's design, its ₹40,000 MRP and Hardlex crystal are difficult to ignore when something like the Zenshin can occasionally be found for less. The Seiko has its own character, and I actually find its dial more consistently appealing in everyday lighting. But if we are comparing what you get for your money, a heavily discounted Zenshin is an extremely convincing alternative.

I would consider anything below ₹40,000 a very attractive deal, provided you like the design. Between ₹40,000 and ₹50,000, I would be a little more selective. At the full ₹65,000, I would personally look at other options before making the decision.

## The Verdict

The Citizen Zenshin Super Titanium Automatic is a watch I find quite difficult to criticise from a technical standpoint. The titanium construction is excellent, the weight is almost comically low for a metal automatic watch, the sapphire crystal and 100 metres of water resistance make it practical, and the dial can look genuinely beautiful when sunlight hits it just right.

But I also think the Zenshin is one of those watches that is slightly more impressive on paper than it is on the wrist. Its specifications create very high expectations, and while the actual watch is certainly good, it never quite gave me that feeling of having discovered something truly exceptional. The dial loses some of its charm indoors, the movement is fairly ordinary, and the overall appearance is more understated than the promotional photographs might lead you to believe.

That does not make it a bad watch. Far from it. In fact, at the ₹35,000 I paid, I think it is a fantastic deal and one of the more compelling titanium automatics you can buy at that price. It just happens to be a watch I appreciate more for what it offers than one I am completely in love with.

Would I recommend it? Absolutely, at the right price. Below ₹40,000, the Zenshin offers a combination of materials, comfort and features that is genuinely difficult to argue with. At its full MRP, I would be considerably less enthusiastic.

Sometimes a watch looks ordinary on paper and turns out to be brilliant in person. The Zenshin is almost the opposite. It looks brilliant on paper and turns out to be a very good watch in person.

And at ₹35,000, very good is more than good enough.
