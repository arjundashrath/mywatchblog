The Great Indian Festival is nearly here, which means the watch section is about to become a sea of crossed-out prices, countdown timers and discounts large enough to make you wonder whether anyone ever paid the original amount. Sometimes there is a properly good watch hiding underneath all of that. Sometimes the watch was cheaper two months ago. We would quite like you to end up with the first kind.

We have put together 18 watches worth keeping an eye on, starting around ₹2,000 and working up to a Seiko GMT. There are familiar Casios, a couple of Timex picks, affordable automatics and some less obvious options from Ratio, Boderry and CIGA Design. The list moves roughly from the affordable end upwards, with a few overlapping price brackets along the way. Having a ₹50,000 budget also does not mean we have to spend every last rupee of it. There are perfectly good watches near the beginning of this list.

You may have noticed quite a lot of black dials here. We tend to start there because they are easy to wear with most things, especially if this is your first watch or the one you will reach for every morning. But do check the other colours before buying. If the version you actually like costs a little more, that can be money better spent than getting the best deal on a watch you feel nothing about.

## 2. The Weekend Doesn't Need a Bigger Budget

### [Timex Weekender T2N654UJ](https://link.amazon/B05iXGzNn) | Buy target: ₹3,800 or less

![Timex Weekender](../../assets/blog/GIF-2026/T2N654.webp)

**The essentials:** 38mm case, quartz movement, fabric strap, Indiglo illumination, 30m water resistance.

The Weekender has a very straightforward appeal. A pale dial, clear numerals and a fabric strap make it look relaxed without looking unfinished, and 38mm is a welcome size when so many inexpensive watches seem determined to cover your entire wrist. We found a ₹3,847 price in May and a later snapshot around ₹4,200. That makes roughly ₹3,800 a sensible sale target. A large discount against the original MRP is less exciting if the checkout price is still what it has been selling for anyway.

The finish is over stainless steel, incidentally. A solid-gold Citizen would require a fairly substantial revision to the title of this article.

The [8210 automatic movement, sapphire crystal and 42-hour reserve](https://www.citizenwatch.com/us/en/product/NJ0152-51X) make it a fairly straightforward watch underneath the colour scheme. The small crown tucked near four o'clock is worth trying in person, especially if you rotate watches and reset them often. We would target ₹32,000–35,000 for this version. If the price stays close to ₹40,000, compare other Tsuyosa colours and retailer offers before paying extra just because this one has caught your eye.

## 18. The GMT, If the Price Comes Back Down

### [Seiko 5 Sports GMT SSK003K1](https://link.amazon/B09HRQv1W) | Buy target: ₹35,000–36,500

The Blueberry is a handsome way to end the list. The blue dial, two-colour bezel and five-row bracelet give it enough detail to feel different from the simpler SRPD, and the extra GMT hand has a useful job if you follow another time zone. The 4R34 is a caller GMT, meaning the GMT hand adjusts independently rather than the local hour hand. That works nicely for keeping track of someone abroad, even if your own travel plans currently extend as far as the office.

This is the most ambitious target here. An older tracked offer reached ₹36,450, but Seiko's official listing now shows ₹55,000, so we should not assume that earlier price will return. We would shortlist it around ₹35,000–36,500 and compare it with other retailers if the offer lands higher. At 13.6mm thick it also has some bulk, and Hardlex remains a compromise at this money. Buy it because you want this particular Seiko GMT, not because the discount has somehow convinced you that you saved enough to buy a second watch.

## Where We Would Start

At the affordable end, we would check the AE-1200 and the two square G-Shocks first. Their recent pricing gives us something useful to work with, and all three have a clear reason to be here. Around ₹10,000–12,000, the Voyager is worth watching if you want an automatic field watch, while the Marlin and my Alba are the ones we would choose largely because we enjoy looking at them. The Citizen Eco-Drive is our more practical pick further up, with the Tsuyosa appealing if the design is what you have been waiting for.

The expensive entries need the discounts to do more work. We would be quite happy to leave a Seiko or CIGA out of the final shopping basket if the price does not land where we want it. Check the exact model, seller, warranty and final payable amount, and compare with the brand's own store or an authorised retailer. Our [guide to buying watches in India](/blog/where-to-buy-watches-in-india/) covers that part in more detail. None of the countdown timers gets to make the decision for you.
