The Great Indian Festival is nearly here, which means the watch section is about to become a sea of crossed-out prices, countdown timers and discounts large enough to make you wonder whether anyone ever paid the original amount. Sometimes there is a properly good watch hiding underneath all of that. Sometimes the watch was cheaper two months ago. We would quite like you to end up with the first kind.

We have put together 18 watches worth keeping an eye on, starting around ₹2,000 and working up to a Seiko GMT. There are familiar Casios, a couple of Timex picks, affordable automatics and some less obvious options from Ratio, Boderry and CIGA Design. Everything is ordered by the price we would want to pay, rather than by brand. Having a ₹50,000 budget also does not mean we have to spend every last rupee of it. There are perfectly good watches near the beginning of this list.

Amazon's [official announcement](https://www.aboutamazon.in/news/retail/amazon-great-indian-festival-2026-dates) gives October 8 as the sale start. The prices below are **buy targets, not confirmed sale offers or predictions**. We used earlier prices and the alternatives available to decide what would make each watch worth recommending. Some targets are more ambitious than others, especially further up the list. This is a researched shortlist, with personal ownership notes on the Alba AX7019X1, rather than a claim that we have worn everything here. Compare the final checkout amount, including any coupon or eligible card discount, before buying.

If you would rather browse without waiting for a sale, our guides to watches [under ₹5,000](/blog/best-watches-under-5k/), [under ₹10,000](/blog/best-watches-under-10k/) and [under ₹20,000](/blog/best-watches-under-20k/) are good places to start. We also have a separate guide to [women's watches under ₹15,000](/blog/womens-watches-under-15k/), with plenty of smaller options.

## 2. The Weekend Doesn't Need a Bigger Budget

### [Timex Weekender T2N654UJ](#buy-link-02) | Buy target: ₹3,800 or less

*Image placeholder: Timex Weekender T2N654UJ on its fabric strap.*
<!-- IMAGE_02 / BUY_LINK_02 -->

**The essentials:** 38mm case, quartz movement, fabric strap, Indiglo illumination, 30m water resistance.

The Weekender has a very straightforward appeal. A pale dial, clear numerals and a fabric strap make it look relaxed without looking unfinished, and 38mm is a welcome size when so many inexpensive watches seem determined to cover your entire wrist. It is the sort of watch we would wear with a T-shirt and then forget to change before going out for dinner. We found a ₹3,847 price in May and a later snapshot around ₹4,200. That makes roughly ₹3,800 a sensible sale target. A large discount against the original MRP is less exciting if the checkout price is still what it has been selling for anyway.

## Where We Would Start

At the affordable end, we would check the AE-1200 and the two square G-Shocks first. Their recent pricing gives us something useful to work with, and all three have a clear reason to be here. Around ₹10,000–12,000, the Voyager is worth watching if you want an automatic field watch, while the Marlin and my Alba are the ones we would choose largely because we enjoy looking at them. The Citizen Eco-Drive is our more practical pick further up, with the Tsuyosa appealing if the design is what you have been waiting for.

The expensive entries need the discounts to do more work. We would be quite happy to leave a Seiko or CIGA out of the final shopping basket if the price does not land where we want it. Check the exact model, seller, warranty and final payable amount, and compare with the brand's own store or an authorised retailer. Our [guide to buying watches in India](/blog/where-to-buy-watches-in-india/) covers that part in more detail. None of the countdown timers gets to make the decision for you.
