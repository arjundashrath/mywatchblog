I have a soft spot for watches that look a little different, and the Timex Q80 is exactly that kind of watch. A gold digital watch with a round dial, an angular case and styling straight out of the 1980s. It is not trying to look modern, sporty or particularly sophisticated. It is just unapologetically retro, and I absolutely love it.

What made the Q80 even more interesting was the price. You can find it for under ₹9,000 in India, and during Flipkart's Big Billion Days sale, the gold version dropped below ₹4,500. That is a pretty ridiculous price for something that looks this distinctive. I went in expecting a fun little retro watch, but what I got was something that felt considerably more premium than I had anticipated. The finishing was excellent, the watch looked even better in person, and the bracelet genuinely caught me off guard.

## Gold, But Not the Kind That Hurts Your Eyes

Let's start with the colour, because I think Timex absolutely nailed it. Gold watches can be a little tricky. Get the shade wrong and you end up with something that looks excessively flashy, the kind of watch that announces its arrival five minutes before you enter the room. Thankfully, the Q80 does not have that problem.

The gold here is a much softer, more subdued shade. It has a lovely warmth to it without being overly bright or yellow. Combined with the brushed finishing, it gives the watch a surprisingly elegant appearance. It still looks unmistakably gold, but in a way that feels tasteful rather than excessive. I honestly did not expect to find a gold digital watch this versatile, but it works beautifully with everyday outfits. Whether I am wearing something casual or dressing up a little, the Q80 somehow fits right in.

Then there is the design itself. The round dial surrounding a rectangular digital display, paired with the more angular outer case, is such an interesting combination. The brushed surface around the display catches the light nicely, while the polished edges add just enough contrast to keep things interesting. It is a design that could easily have looked awkward, but everything comes together beautifully. The result is a watch that looks properly vintage without feeling like a cheap retro novelty.

## The Bracelet Is Ridiculously Good

I did not expect to be writing an entire section praising the bracelet of a relatively affordable Timex digital watch, but here we are.

This was easily the biggest surprise for me. The stainless steel bracelet feels sturdy, substantial and genuinely well made. There is a reassuring weight to it, and the finishing is far better than I expected. It does not have that flimsy, rattly feeling that you sometimes get with affordable metal bracelets. In fact, I have handled watches costing considerably more that did not feel nearly as good on the wrist.

And that is what makes it so impressive. At this price, I would have been perfectly happy with a bracelet that simply looked good and did its job. Instead, Timex has given the Q80 something that genuinely adds to the experience of wearing it. The brushed gold finish continues beautifully from the case into the bracelet, giving the whole watch a cohesive, almost integrated appearance. It feels like a proper part of the design rather than something added at the end to keep the costs down.

Someone at Timex clearly forgot that this was supposed to be an affordable digital watch.

There is one annoyance, though. **The bracelet has no micro-adjustment**, which means getting that absolutely perfect fit can be a little difficult. Depending on your wrist, you might find yourself choosing between slightly tighter and slightly looser. It is not a dealbreaker for me, but with how good the bracelet is otherwise, having some additional adjustment would have been lovely.

## How Does It Wear?

The Q80 has a 39mm stainless steel case with a thickness of just 9.5mm, and those proportions work really well with its design. It is relatively slim, sits close to the wrist and does not feel like a chunky digital sports watch. The angular case gives it a little more visual presence than the dimensions might suggest, but the overall proportions are nicely balanced.

What I particularly enjoy is how naturally it sits on the wrist. The relatively low profile and the bracelet make it comfortable to wear throughout the day, while the design is interesting enough that I keep looking down at it. Not because I need to check the time, but because I just like looking at the watch. That is usually a pretty good sign.

There is also something refreshing about wearing a digital watch that does not look like it belongs in a gym. I love my G-Shocks, but the Q80 offers a completely different experience. It has the convenience of a digital display while looking more like a piece of vintage jewellery or an old-school dress watch. It is fun, a little unusual and surprisingly easy to wear.

## Simple Functions, and That Is Perfectly Fine

The Q80 uses a quartz digital movement and gives you the usual essentials, including a calendar, alarm and stopwatch. There is nothing particularly groundbreaking about the functionality, but I do not think there needs to be. It tells the time, provides the basic digital features you would expect and gets on with its job.

The metal buttons deserve a mention too. They feel nice to press, and their finishing matches the case well. It is a small detail, but on a watch where the overall construction feels so good, having proper metal pushers helps maintain that impression.

You also get Timex's **INDIGLO backlight**, which works well and makes the display easy to read in the dark. I do have one small complaint about how it operates, though. On my Q80, the light stays illuminated only while the button is being held down. The moment I release it, the light goes off. I would have preferred it to remain on for a couple of seconds after a press, as that would make checking the time in the dark a little more convenient.

It is a minor nitpick, and certainly not something that would stop me from recommending the watch. Still, it is one of those little things you notice during everyday use.

On paper, there is nothing especially extraordinary here. Mineral glass, a standard digital movement and 50 metres of water resistance are perfectly reasonable for an everyday watch. This is not something I would buy for swimming or outdoor adventures, and it is certainly not competing with a G-Shock on functionality.

But that is also missing the point of the Q80. The appeal is not how many functions Timex has managed to squeeze into it. It is how well the whole watch has been put together, particularly its case, finishing and bracelet.

## A Much Cheaper Alternative to the Tissot PRX Digital?

If you like the general idea of the Tissot PRX Digital but cannot justify spending around ₹40,000 on a digital watch, I think the Q80 is a fantastic alternative to consider.

Now, I am not saying they are the same watch. The Tissot has its own design, materials and finishing, and it sits in a completely different price category. But both offer that unmistakable retro digital aesthetic with a metal bracelet and a design that feels more like a fashion statement than a conventional digital sports watch.

What makes the Q80 so appealing is how much of that experience you get for a fraction of the money. The brushed metal, geometric case, retro display and excellent bracelet all come together to create something that feels far more special than its price suggests. If the main reason you are attracted to the PRX Digital is its vintage styling rather than the Tissot name, the Timex is absolutely worth looking at.

## Is It Worth ₹9,000? What About ₹4,500?

Here is where the Q80 becomes particularly interesting. Its listed retail price in India is around ₹12,000, but it can be found for less than ₹9,000, with sales occasionally taking it considerably lower. During Flipkart's Big Billion Days, the gold version went for under ₹4,500, which is an absurdly good deal considering what you are getting.

Would I recommend it even without a huge discount? Absolutely. The design is distinctive, the finishing is excellent, and that bracelet alone makes the watch feel more expensive than it is. At under ₹9,000, I think it offers a really enjoyable ownership experience. Even closer to its listed retail price, I can see the appeal if you particularly love the design, although I would definitely check for discounts before paying full price.

At ₹4,500, though? It is almost unfair. You are getting a stainless steel digital watch with excellent finishing, a surprisingly premium bracelet, INDIGLO and a design that stands out from just about everything else in that price range. There are watches with more features, better water resistance and tougher construction, but very few that look quite like this.

## The Verdict

The Timex Q80 is one of those watches that reminds me why I enjoy collecting watches in the first place. It does not have an impressive mechanical movement, an exotic material or a long list of complications. What it does have is a fantastic design, lovely finishing and a personality that is difficult to find in affordable watches.

I love the softer gold colour, the combination of the round dial and angular case, and just how good it looks with everyday clothes. The bracelet is easily the biggest surprise, feeling more substantial and better finished than bracelets on some much more expensive watches. The lack of micro-adjustment is annoying, and I wish the INDIGLO light stayed on a little longer, but those are relatively minor complaints when the rest of the watch gets so much right.

**Would I recommend the Timex Q80? Without hesitation.** It is unique, beautifully retro and feels more premium than I ever expected it to. I would recommend it at its normal selling price, and if you happen to catch it during a major sale, it becomes an even easier purchase.

Not every watch needs to be a technical marvel. Sometimes it just needs to look fantastic, feel great on your wrist and make you smile whenever you check the time.

The Q80 does all three.
