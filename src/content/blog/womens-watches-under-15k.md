We have spent quite a bit of time finding watches [under ₹5,000](https://thewristjournal.com/blog/best-watches-under-5k/), [under ₹10,000](https://thewristjournal.com/blog/best-watches-under-10k/) and, more recently, [under ₹20,000](https://thewristjournal.com/blog/best-watches-under-20k/). This time, we are looking at the part of the watch counter that usually gets described with some combination of “elegant,” “timeless” and “perfect for her.” Which tells you approximately nothing about the watch. There are some lovely options here, from tiny gold digitals and proper sports watches to dials that deserve much better product photographs.

Before we start, all watches can be worn by anyone. We have said this before and will happily keep saying it. These are mostly watches brands put in their women's collections, alongside a few that fit the brief particularly well. There is no reason to avoid something because the website has filed it under the wrong gender. Pay more attention to the size, strap fit and whether you actually like looking at it. Your wrist has not read the marketing material.

The picks below run from cheapest to most expensive, with two clearly marked options just above ₹15,000 at the end. This is a researched buying guide built around our shortlist and design preferences, rather than a claim that we have worn every watch here. Prices marked **shortlisted offer** are the deals used to build the list, not guaranteed current checkout prices; **MRP** means the brand's listed price. Offers and stock change, and some Casios are easier to find at an offline counter. We have checked offline availability while putting the shortlist together, but call your local store before making the trip.

## Where We Would Start

For a first everyday watch, the Casio LTP-1302DS would be near the top of our shortlist. The mother-of-pearl dial gives it enough character to stay interesting, while the price leaves you room to buy something else later. The little LTP-V007 is the dressier budget choice, and the Milano is particularly appealing if you want a watch that also feels like jewellery. If sport and swimming are part of the brief, we would go straight to the G-LIDE or smaller CasiOak. There is very little point buying a delicate leather-strapped watch and then worrying about it through every workout.

With a little more to spend, the Sheen SHE-4550D-2A is probably the one we would investigate first. That dial is lovely, the size is sensible and sapphire is a useful extra at the price. The Swatch Caramellissima sits at the other end of the decision: you either want a candy bracelet that tells the time or you do not. We rather like that there is room for both. You do not have to choose the most serious watch you can afford, and having ₹15,000 available does not oblige you to spend all of it.
